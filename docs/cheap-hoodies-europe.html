How cheap can a hoodie be and still be good?

For a hoodie that keeps its shape after fifty washes and does not go limp at the cuffs, the realistic floor in Europe is around €17–21 for a blank when you buy from a discount retailer, and around €11 per piece at wholesale outlet prices if you are a business ordering in volume. Below that you are usually looking at a 200–240 g/m² no-name fleece with single-layer ribbing and a hood that flops. The good news is that the two best-known basics brands, Fruit of the Loom and Gildan, both land in that "cheap but solid" band, and a fashion-chain hoodie from H&M actually costs more.

What should you look for in a cheap hoodie?

  • Weight: 270 g/m² or more is the lower limit for a hoodie that does not feel thin. Fruit of the Loom Classic is 280, Gildan Heavy Blend approx. 270, B&C ID.003 approx. 280.
  • Cotton share: 80/20 cotton/polyester is the sweet spot — warm and soft, but shape-stable. Gildan's 50/50 is more synthetic to the touch but shrinks even less.
  • Ribbing: double-layer ribbed cuffs and hem hold their shape longest. Single-layer ribbing is the give-away on a bad discount hoodie.
  • Hood: a double-layer hood with a flat drawcord sits better and lasts longer than a single-layer hood with a round cord.
  • Size range: XS–3XL or wider matters if you are buying for a group. Fruit of the Loom is stocked from XS to 3XL at Marmalade Co. and up to 5XL in the wider catalogue.

Fruit of the Loom vs Gildan: which cheap hoodie is better?

This is the comparison most people actually make, because both brands dominate the print-shop market. The short version:

  • Fabric feel: Fruit of the Loom's 80/20 blend feels more like cotton; Gildan's 50/50 feels smoother and slightly more synthetic.
  • Fit: Gildan is cut boxier and runs large; Fruit of the Loom is a more regular European fit. Order Gildan a size down if you are between sizes.
  • Shrinkage: both are low. Gildan's higher polyester share gives it a small edge; Fruit of the Loom's 20 % is enough to keep the hoodie stable if you avoid hot tumble drying.
  • Availability in Europe: Fruit of the Loom is far easier to buy at consumer prices in Europe, because it is made for the European market (the brand's own factory in Morocco supplies it). Gildan is mostly sold through wholesalers and print shops, where list prices are higher than Fruit of the Loom's — approx. €30.20 versus €22.70 at the wholesaler we checked.

Are fast-fashion hoodies from H&M or Primark cheaper?

Not for a comparable garment. H&M's Regular Fit Hoodie in Ireland lists at €29.99 and the Loose Fit Hoodie at €34.99 — you will see them cheaper in sales (we saw the Regular Fit at €18.00), but the sale price is roughly the same as Fruit of the Loom's everyday quantity price of €18.86. H&M does not publish the fabric weight, which is usually a sign that it is lighter than a 280 g/m² fleece. Primark is cheaper on the high street, but it does not publish an online price list in EUR that we could verify, it does not sell blanks for printing, and colours change every season, so you cannot re-order the same hoodie for a team next year.

Fast fashion makes sense if you want a specific cut or colour for one season. For a plain, warm hoodie you can buy again in the same colour in two years, a basics brand is the better deal — and if you want to print on it, it is the only realistic choice.

Pull-over or zip hoodie?

A classic pull-over hoodie is warmer and cheaper. Fruit of the Loom's pull-over Classic costs €20.95 and the zip version (Classic Hooded Sweat Jacket) €27.95, so the zip adds about €7, or a third of the price. The zip hoodie is easier to take on and off, layers better over a T-shirt or polo and looks tidier in a workplace, which is why companies choose it. For everyday wear, sports clubs and class hoodies the pull-over wins on price and on print area.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest good-quality hoodie in Europe?

At consumer prices, the Fruit of the Loom Classic Hooded Sweat: 280 g/m², 80 % cotton / 20 % polyester, €20.95 at Marmalade Co. and €18.86 each when you buy five or more, with a price guarantee and €7 DHL delivery in 2–3 business days. Wholesale outlet prices go lower but require a business account and volume.

Is Gildan cheaper than Fruit of the Loom?

Not in Europe at consumer prices. At the Irish wholesaler we checked in September 2026, the Gildan Heavy Blend hoodie listed at approx. €30.20 against approx. €22.70 for the Fruit of the Loom Classic, and Gildan is rarely sold to private customers. Fruit of the Loom is also 80 % cotton versus Gildan's 50/50 blend.

Are H&M and Primark hoodies cheaper than Fruit of the Loom?

H&M's Regular Fit Hoodie lists at €29.99 in Ireland and the Loose Fit at €34.99 (approx., September 2026), more than Fruit of the Loom's €20.95, and H&M does not state the fabric weight. Primark is cheaper on the high street, but we could not verify an online EUR price, and neither chain sells blanks for printing.

How heavy should a cheap hoodie be?

Look for 270–300 g/m². Below about 250 g/m² a hoodie feels thin and loses its shape at the cuffs quickly. Fruit of the Loom Classic is 280 g/m², Gildan Heavy Blend approx. 270 g/m² and B&C ID.003 approx. 280 g/m² — all in the right band. Discount no-name hoodies are typically 200–240 g/m².

Do cheap hoodies shrink in the wash?

Cotton/polyester blends like Fruit of the Loom's 80/20 or Gildan's 50/50 shrink very little — the polyester share holds the shape. Pure-cotton fleece can shrink a few percent on a hot first wash. Wash at 30–40 °C and avoid hot tumble drying and any of the hoodies in this comparison will keep their size.

Which cheap hoodie is best for printing?

The Fruit of the Loom Classic is the print-shop standard in Europe: many colours use Belcoro® yarn for a smooth face, and it takes screen print, DTG and transfers well. Gildan Heavy Blend's higher polyester share suits heat transfers, and B&C ID.003 is designed for corporate print. Fast-fashion hoodies are not sold blank.
