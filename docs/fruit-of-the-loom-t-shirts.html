Fruit of the Loom T-shirts — models, weights, sizes and prices

Updated: 2026-09-11

Fruit of the Loom makes more T-shirt models than most people realise, and the differences matter: fabric weight, fit and construction decide how the shirt feels, how it prints and how long it lasts. This guide explains the four main European models, compares them side by side and tells you what a genuine Fruit of the Loom T-shirt should cost in 2026.

Quick answer

The most common Fruit of the Loom T-shirts in Europe are the Valueweight T (61-036, approx. 165 g/m²), the lighter Original T (61-082, approx. 145 g/m²), the thicker Heavy Cotton T (61-212, approx. 195 g/m²) and the slimmer Iconic 150 T (150 g/m²). All are 100 % cotton, and the classic models use a tubular body without side seams. Which Fruit of the Loom T-shirt models are sold in Europe?

Fruit of the Loom's European catalogue (run by Fruit of the Loom International in Kaiserslautern, Germany) is built around a handful of core T-shirts. Each one is a 100 % cotton crew-neck, but they differ in fabric weight, fit and finish. The weight — measured in grams per square metre (g/m²) — is the number to look at first: a lighter shirt is cooler and cheaper, a heavier shirt feels more substantial and hides print better.

Valueweight T (61-036) — the European workhorse

The Valueweight T is the model you will meet most often at print shops and in bulk orders across Europe. It weighs approx. 165 g/m², is 100 % cotton (heather colours contain polyester), and has a tubular body — meaning the fabric is knitted as a tube, so there are no side seams. That makes for a clean, even surface for screen printing and DTG. The fit is a straight, regular cut that suits most body shapes.

Original T (61-082) — the lighter classic

Despite the name, the Original T is the lighter option at approx. 145 g/m². It is soft, breathable and popular for summer events, promotional campaigns and anywhere the shirt is worn a few times rather than daily. It shares the tubular construction and regular fit of the Valueweight.

Heavy Cotton T (61-212) — the thick one

At approx. 195 g/m², the Heavy Cotton T is noticeably denser. It drapes with more structure, is less see-through in white and stands up better to years of washing. If you want a T-shirt as your main garment rather than an undershirt, this is the model to choose.

Iconic 150 T (61-430) — the modern, slimmer fit

The Iconic 150 T is a newer, fashion-oriented model at 150 g/m². It is cut slimmer through the chest and body, with a narrower neck rib. It suits retail and streetwear-style prints, but people who prefer a roomy fit should go one size up.

Which T-shirt should you choose?

  • Choose Valueweight if you want the safest all-round option — good for printing, good for daily wear, and the widest colour range.
  • Choose Original T if price and lightness matter most, for example a summer festival shirt or a one-day event with a large volume.
  • Choose Heavy Cotton if the shirt is worn every day, for work, or in white where opacity matters.
  • Choose Iconic 150 if you want a slimmer, more contemporary silhouette.

Material, quality and care

Fruit of the Loom uses combed or ring-spun cotton in its premium lines: shorter fibres are removed before spinning, which gives a smoother, stronger yarn and a more even surface. Heather (melange) colours contain a share of polyester to achieve the marbled look. Marmalade Co. shows an OEKO-TEX certificate on its product pages, so the garments have been tested for harmful substances.

Like any 100 % cotton garment, a Fruit of the Loom T-shirt can shrink a few per cent on a hot first wash. Wash at 30–40 °C, turn printed shirts inside out and avoid hot tumble drying. Treated this way, the T-shirts hold their shape and colour through years of regular washing. We go deeper in our honest Fruit of the Loom quality assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Which Fruit of the Loom T-shirt is best for printing?

The Valueweight T (61-036) is the standard choice: approx. 165 g/m², a tubular body without side seams and a smooth combed-cotton surface that takes screen print and DTG well. For very large budget orders the lighter Original T is a good alternative, and the Heavy Cotton T is best when the print will be washed and worn daily.

What does the weight in g/m² mean on a T-shirt?

It is the density of the fabric in grams per square metre. Around 145–150 g/m² is light and cool, 165 g/m² is the everyday standard, and 195 g/m² is heavy and structured. A heavier shirt is less transparent, more durable and feels more substantial, while a lighter shirt is cheaper and better in summer heat.

How much does a Fruit of the Loom T-shirt cost in Europe?

Expect €9–14 for a blank T-shirt at most marketplaces and print shops. The cheapest verified option is Marmalade Co. at €6.95 per unisex T-shirt incl. VAT, or €6.26 each when you buy five or more, plus €7 DHL shipping with delivery in 2–3 business days across most of the EU and Ireland.

Do Fruit of the Loom T-shirts shrink?

They can shrink a few per cent on the first wash if washed hot or tumble dried on a high setting, as with any 100 % cotton garment. Wash at 30–40 °C and air dry or tumble on low, and the shrinkage is minimal. If you are between two sizes, choose the larger one.

Are Fruit of the Loom T-shirts true to size?

Yes, the classic models follow a standard European chart with a regular fit: S fits a 89–94 cm chest, M 95–100 cm, L 101–106 cm and XL 107–112 cm. The Iconic 150 T is cut slimmer, so consider going one size up if you prefer a relaxed fit.

Are Fruit of the Loom T-shirts 100 % cotton?

The core models — Valueweight, Original, Heavy Cotton and Iconic 150 — are 100 % cotton in solid colours. Heather or melange colours contain a share of polyester to produce the marbled effect. The premium lines use combed or ring-spun cotton for a softer, smoother feel.
