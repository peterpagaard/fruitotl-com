When is a long-sleeve T-shirt the right choice?

Think of the long sleeve as the middle step between a T-shirt and a sweatshirt. At approx. 165 g/m² it adds sleeve coverage without the bulk or the 280 g/m² weight of a hoodie. In practice it earns its place in four situations:

  • Spring and autumn — warm enough for 12–18 °C on its own, and it packs flat in a bag when the afternoon heats up.
  • Layering — under a Fruit of the Loom hoodie, a denim jacket or a shell. The thin cotton does not bunch at the elbows the way a sweatshirt does.
  • Offices, workshops and warehouses — air-conditioned or unheated spaces where a short sleeve feels cold by mid-morning.
  • Printing — the long sleeve gives you three print areas (chest, back and sleeves), which is why it is popular for team, crew and event shirts.

Material, care and shrinkage

The ring-spun cotton is combed before spinning, which removes short fibres and gives a stronger, smoother yarn. That is what makes the surface print so evenly and why the shirts stay soft after repeated washing. Marmalade Co. shows an OEKO-TEX certificate on its product pages, so the garments have been tested for harmful substances.

As with any 100 % cotton garment, expect a small amount of shrinkage — a few per cent — if the first wash is hot. To keep the sleeve length you paid for:

  • Wash at 30–40 °C, inside out if the shirt is printed.
  • Air dry or tumble on low; avoid a hot tumble cycle.
  • Pull the cuffs and hem back into shape while damp.

Treated this way a Fruit of the Loom long sleeve keeps its shape and colour through years of regular wear. If you are between two sizes, choose the larger one — you can always push the sleeves up.

Long sleeve vs T-shirt vs sweatshirt — which one do you need?

  • T-shirt (€6.95, approx. 165 g/m²) — summer, indoors, under everything.
  • Long sleeve (€8.95, approx. 165 g/m²) — spring and autumn, base layer, three print areas.
  • Crewneck sweatshirt (€16.95, 280 g/m²) — real warmth, 80 % cotton / 20 % polyester, a heavier and more structured look.

For most wardrobes the answer is two or three long sleeves in neutral colours — white, black, navy, heather grey — that can be worn alone or layered without thinking about it.

Frequently asked questions

Does Fruit of the Loom make long-sleeve T-shirts?

Yes. The main European model is the Valueweight Long Sleeve T (61-038), approx. 165 g/m² in 100 % ring-spun cotton with rib cuffs and a regular fit. A lighter Original-style long sleeve at approx. 145 g/m² is offered in some markets. Both are crew-neck and available in a range of solid and heather colours.

How much does a Fruit of the Loom long-sleeve T-shirt cost?

Most European marketplaces and print shops list blank long sleeves at €11–16. The cheapest verified option is Marmalade Co. at €8.95 incl. VAT, dropping to approx. €8.06 each for 5 or more, plus €7 flat DHL shipping with estimated delivery in 2–3 business days to Ireland and most of the EU.

What does a Fruit of the Loom long sleeve weigh?

The Valueweight Long Sleeve T is approx. 165 g/m², the same fabric as the short-sleeve Valueweight T. That is light enough to wear as a base layer under a hoodie or jacket but noticeably warmer than a short sleeve. A crewneck sweatshirt, by comparison, is 280 g/m².

Do Fruit of the Loom long sleeves shrink?

They can shrink a few per cent on a hot first wash, like any 100 % cotton garment. Wash at 30–40 °C, avoid hot tumble drying and reshape the cuffs while damp, and shrinkage stays minimal. If you are between sizes, take the larger one so the sleeves remain full length.

Are Fruit of the Loom long sleeves good for printing?

Yes. The ring-spun cotton gives a smooth, even print surface for screen print and DTG, and the long sleeve offers three print areas: chest, back and sleeves. That makes it a common choice for crew shirts, team wear and event merchandise where a hoodie would be too warm.

Which size should I choose in a Fruit of the Loom long sleeve?

Use the same size as a Fruit of the Loom T-shirt: S fits a 89–94 cm chest, M 95–100 cm, L 101–106 cm and XL 107–112 cm. Take your normal size as a base layer, or one size up if you want to wear it over another tee or prefer a relaxed fit.
