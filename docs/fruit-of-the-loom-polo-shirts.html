Fruit of the Loom polo shirts — models, piqué, 100 % cotton vs 65/35

A polo shirt sits in the gap between a T-shirt and a proper shirt, which is why it is the default uniform for shops, hospitality, trades and golf clubs across Europe. Fruit of the Loom makes several piqué polos, and the real decision is fabric: 100 % cotton for feel, or a 65/35 polyester-cotton blend for colour fastness and easy care. This guide compares them and tells you what to pay in 2026.

Which polo shirts does Fruit of the Loom make?

All Fruit of the Loom polos are piqué knits — the small waffle-like texture that gives a polo its characteristic look and makes it more breathable than flat jersey. The European catalogue centres on two fabrics and a few fit variants:

Premium Polo (63-218) — 100 % cotton piqué

The Premium Polo is knitted from 100 % cotton at approx. 180 g/m². It has the softest hand of the range, a ribbed collar and cuffs, a three-button placket with reinforced neck tape and side vents at the hem. Being pure cotton it breathes well and feels most like a "proper" polo, which makes it the choice for customer-facing staff, gifts and anyone wearing it for comfort rather than as a uniform.

65/35 Polo (63-402) — polyester-cotton blend

The 65/35 Polo uses 65 % polyester / 35 % cotton piqué at a similar weight. The polyester share makes it more colour-fast, quicker to dry, less prone to shrinking and better at keeping its shape after hundreds of washes. That is why it dominates in workwear: warehouses, cleaning companies, catering and trade uniforms that get washed at 60 °C every week.

Original and Lady-Fit variants

Retailers also stock an "Original" polo — the everyday cotton-rich version sold, for example, by Marmalade Co. at €10.95 — and Lady-Fit polos with a shaped waist and shorter sleeves for women. Sizing and construction otherwise follow the main models.

100 % cotton or 65/35 — which should you choose?

  • Choose 100 % cotton (Premium) when feel and appearance matter most: reception staff, sales teams, club wear, or a polo you will wear on your own time. It is softer, breathes better and looks slightly more upmarket.
  • Choose 65/35 when the polo is a working garment: it survives 60 °C washes, dries fast, resists fading in strong colours and does not need ironing. For a uniform washed weekly for a year, the blend will simply look newer.
  • Printing vs embroidery: both fabrics embroider well. For transfer printing the blend's smoother, more stable surface is slightly easier to work with.

Polo shirt sizes and fit

Fruit of the Loom polos use the same regular-fit size chart as the brand's T-shirts. The ribbed collar and placket add a little structure, so the polo looks slightly slimmer than a T-shirt in the same size without actually being tighter. If you like a polo to sit close to the body, stay with your usual size; for a relaxed fit or if you are between sizes, go one up.

Polos with embroidery or print

The polo is the most embroidered garment in corporate wear, and piqué is well suited to it: the textured knit hides needle holes and the collar frames a left-chest logo neatly. Typical placements are the left chest (logo, 8–10 cm wide), the sleeve (a small secondary mark) and, less often, the upper back. Embroidery costs more per unit than print but lasts the life of the garment and looks more professional on a uniform. Transfer or screen printing is cheaper for large runs and works well on the 65/35 blend. Our workwear with logo guide compares the costs per employee.

Washing a polo so the collar keeps its shape

The ribbed collar is the first thing to age on a cheap polo. To keep it flat and firm: wash at 30–40 °C (the 65/35 blend tolerates 60 °C when needed), button the top button before washing so the placket does not twist, air dry on a hanger or tumble on low, and iron the collar flat from the underside while it is slightly damp. Avoid wringing the collar and never dry a cotton polo on a hot cycle.

Frequently asked questions

Which Fruit of the Loom polo is best for workwear?

The 65/35 Polo (63-402). Its 65 % polyester / 35 % cotton piqué holds colour through hot washes, dries quickly, resists shrinking and rarely needs ironing, which matters when a uniform is washed every week. The 100 % cotton Premium Polo feels softer but ages faster under that kind of use.

How much does a Fruit of the Loom polo shirt cost?

Expect €13–20 for a blank polo at most European retailers. The cheapest verified option is Marmalade Co. at €10.95 incl. VAT for the Original polo, with 5–10 % quantity discounts from three pieces and €7 DHL shipping with delivery in 2–3 business days to Ireland and most EU countries.

Is the Fruit of the Loom Premium Polo 100 % cotton?

Yes. The Premium Polo (63-218) is 100 % cotton piqué at approx. 180 g/m², with a ribbed collar and cuffs and a three-button placket. It is the softest and most breathable polo in the range, and the one to choose when comfort and a smarter look matter more than easy care.

Do Fruit of the Loom polos fit the same as the T-shirts?

Yes, the men's and unisex polos use the same regular-fit chart: S 89–94 cm chest, M 95–100 cm, L 101–106 cm, XL 107–112 cm. The collar and placket make them look slightly slimmer, but the body is the same. Lady-Fit polos are cut smaller with a shaped waist.

Can Fruit of the Loom polos be embroidered?

Yes, and they are among the most embroidered garments in Europe. The piqué texture hides needle holes and a left-chest logo of 8–10 cm looks neat under the collar. Both the 100 % cotton and the 65/35 fabric embroider well; for transfer printing the blend's more stable surface is slightly easier.
