Why screen printing gets cheap at 100 pieces

Screen printing has a fixed cost per colour — a screen has to be exposed and the press set up — and a small cost per shirt. On 10 shirts the set-up dominates; on 100 it is spread thin. That is why printers usually quote a minimum of 10–25 pieces per design and why the price per shirt drops sharply between 25 and 100 pieces. Three rules keep the bill down:

  • Fewer colours. Every extra ink colour adds a screen, another set-up fee and roughly €1 per shirt. A strong one- or two-colour design is the cheapest route.
  • Dark shirts cost more. Printing on black or navy needs a white underbase, which counts as an extra colour — roughly €0.85–1 more per shirt plus one more set-up fee on the price lists we checked.
  • One print position. A second position (back print, sleeve) is priced as a new job with its own set-up.

Full-colour photos or gradients are cheaper as DTF or DTG transfers, which have no set-up but a higher price per piece. Our guide to clothing for printing compares the techniques in detail.

Which Fruit of the Loom t-shirt for a 100-piece order?

For almost every 100-piece job the Valueweight T (61-036) is the right shirt: approx. 165 g/m², 100 % cotton, knitted as a tube without side seams, which gives an uninterrupted print area and nothing for a screen to catch on. It is the shirt most European printers keep on the shelf, and it is the one behind the prices above. If the shirts will be sold as merch and feel matters more than price, the heavier Heavy Cotton T (61-212, approx. 195 g/m²) is the usual upgrade; for a lighter summer tee the Original T (61-082, approx. 145 g/m²) exists, but availability at retail is patchier. Check fit with the size guide before splitting sizes.

How to split 100 shirts across sizes

Collect sizes from the wearers wherever you can. If you must guess — for merch, spares or a mixed adult crowd — a common starting split for 100 unisex tees is roughly S 12, M 28, L 28, XL 18, XXL 10 and XXXL 4. Add one or two XS if women or teenagers are included. Fruit of the Loom tees are a regular unisex fit (chest in cm: S 89–94, M 95–100, L 101–106, XL 107–112), and 100 % cotton can shrink a few percent on a hot first wash, so print on unwashed shirts and wash at 30–40 °C afterwards.

Blank from a shop or from a wholesaler?

Textile wholesalers can undercut retail on the garment itself, but usually only for VAT-registered businesses, often with net prices, minimum order values and shipping only within their own country. Once VAT and cross-border freight are added, a retail shop with an automatic quantity discount is often the cheaper and simpler route for 100 pieces. Checklist before you order 100 t-shirts

  1. Choose one model — normally the Valueweight T — and one or two colours.
  2. Collect sizes, then add two or three spares in M and L.
  3. Ask the printer for a quote with set-up fees, VAT and delivery included.
  4. Order blanks 1–2 weeks before the printer needs them: delivery is 2–3 business days, printing often one to two weeks.
  5. Compare quotes on the same basis: price per finished shirt, delivered.

How much do 100 printed t-shirts cost?

With blank Fruit of the Loom tees at approx. €632.50 delivered and a one-colour screen print at roughly €200 net including set-up, 100 printed t-shirts land at around €830, or about €8.30 per shirt before VAT on the printing. Three colours, DTF transfers or embroidery raise the total to roughly €1,080–1,480 on the price lists we checked in September 2026.

Is it cheaper to buy blanks and print them separately?

At 100 pieces it usually is. Blank Fruit of the Loom tees cost €6.26 each from 5 pieces at Marmalade Co., and a local screen printer adds roughly €2 per shirt for a one-colour design. One-stop print services that supply shirt and print together list printed tees from about €15 at small quantities, so get a volume quote from both before deciding.

What is the cheapest printing method for 100 t-shirts?

For a design with one to three solid colours, screen printing is normally cheapest at 100 pieces, because the set-up fee is spread over many shirts: roughly €1.55 per shirt plus about €45 set-up for one colour on the lists we checked. For full-colour artwork, DTF transfers at about €4.50 per shirt with no set-up are often cheaper than a four-colour screen print.

Do I get a discount if I mix sizes and colours?

Yes. At Marmalade Co. the quantity discount applies per product, so all sizes and colours of the same Fruit of the Loom t-shirt count together: 5 % off from 3 pieces and 10 % off from 5 pieces. The V-neck, the long-sleeve tee and the tank top are separate products and count separately.

How long does an order of 100 t-shirts take?

Printing adds the printer's lead time, typically one to two weeks for screen printing and longer in busy months such as September to November, so plan three weeks from order to finished shirts.
