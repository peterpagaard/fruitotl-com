Does Fruit of the Loom sell underwear in Europe?

Yes, but the range looks different from the one Americans know. In the United States, Fruit of the Loom is first and foremost an underwear brand — multipacks of boxer briefs, briefs and undershirts have been supermarket staples for generations, and the label itself dates back to 1871, one of the oldest trademarks in the country. In Europe, where the brand is run by Fruit of the Loom International from Kaiserslautern in Germany, the catalogue is built around T-shirts, sweats and polos for retail and printing. Underwear and socks are sold as basics alongside that range: plain cotton underwear in small packs, unisex socks and tank tops and tees that double as undershirts.

That is worth knowing before you search, because the big US-style 6-packs of boxer briefs are mostly not what you will find on European shelves. What you will find is cheap, plain, cotton-rich basics from the same factory network that makes the tees.

How does Fruit of the Loom underwear compare with other brands?

The brand's pitch has not changed in 170 years: plain, comfortable cotton basics at a low price, made in large volumes so the quality is consistent from pack to pack. Compared with designer underwear from brands such as Calvin Klein or Tommy Hilfiger, you are paying a fraction of the price and giving up mainly the logo on the waistband; day-to-day comfort in a cotton-rich fabric is comparable. Compared with supermarket and fast-fashion own-brand packs, Fruit of the Loom's advantage is consistency — the same cut, the same colours and the same sizes are available year after year, so you can re-order the pair you like.

  • Best for: people who want plain black or grey cotton basics at around €5 per pair and do not care about branding.
  • Not for: anyone who wants a wide choice of prints, patterns, sports-specific trunks or premium modal fabrics — that is not what this range is.

How should you wash Fruit of the Loom underwear and socks?

  • Wash at 40 °C with ordinary detergent; avoid bleach, which weakens elastic.
  • Separate dark and light. Black socks and underwear with the dark load, white tank tops with the whites.
  • Skip the hot tumble dryer. Heat is what kills elastic. Air drying or a low setting doubles the life of a waistband.
  • No ironing needed. Cotton underwear and socks come out of a normal spin fine.
  • Replace on a schedule. When a waistband loosens or a sock heel goes thin, it is time — at €2.95 per pair there is no reason to keep worn-out socks.

Is Fruit of the Loom underwear 100 % cotton?

The brand's basics are cotton-rich, and premium lines use combed cotton with an OEKO-TEX certificate shown on product pages. Underwear with a stretch waistband typically contains a small share of elastane, so check the composition on the product page for the exact pack. Tank tops and the Valueweight T used as undershirts are 100 % cotton.

What size Fruit of the Loom underwear should I buy?

Take the size you wear in the brand's T-shirts: S fits an 89–94 cm chest, M 95–100 cm, L 101–106 cm and XL 107–112 cm, which correspond roughly to waists of 76–82, 82–90, 90–98 and 98–106 cm. If you are between sizes, go up for underwear. Socks are sold by EU shoe size band, for example 43–46.

How do I stop underwear elastic from wearing out?

Heat is the main enemy. Wash at 40 °C without bleach, avoid hot tumble drying and air dry instead, and wash darks separately so black underwear and socks keep their colour. Treated this way the waistband outlasts the fabric. Replace a pair when the band loosens — at these prices there is no reason to keep worn-out basics.
