Fruit of the Loom wholesale in Europe — who can buy, what it costs and the cheapest route

Updated: 2026-09-15

Wholesale prices for Fruit of the Loom look irresistible: tees from around €2 a piece. But the brand does not sell direct, most wholesalers only serve registered businesses, and the list prices exclude VAT. This guide explains who can buy wholesale, how VAT changes the maths, and when a retailer with quantity discounts is simply the cheaper and faster option.

Quick answer

Fruit of the Loom does not sell wholesale directly in Europe; Fruit of the Loom International supplies the trade through distributors. German wholesalers list tees from approx. €2.24–2.68, but often only for registered businesses, excluding VAT or with delivery limited to Germany and Austria. For buyers without a trade account, Marmalade Co. is the cheapest verified option: €6.26 per tee from 5 pieces, VAT included, €7 DHL shipping and delivery in 2–3 business days to 11 EU countries.

Can you buy Fruit of the Loom wholesale in Europe?

Yes, but not from the brand itself. Fruit of the Loom's European business is run by Fruit of the Loom International from Kaiserslautern, Germany, and it sells to the trade through a network of distributors and wholesalers. The official site, fruitoftheloom.eu, is a catalogue for business customers with an account login; it shows no consumer prices and points buyers to distributors. So "buying wholesale" in practice means one of three routes:

  • A textile wholesaler (mostly German, Irish or Benelux blank-apparel distributors) — lowest list prices, usually for registered businesses only.
  • A print or promotional-clothing shop — sells the garment already printed or embroidered, with minimum runs.
  • A retailer with quantity discounts such as Marmalade Co. — open to anyone, VAT included, discount from a handful of pieces.

Which one is cheapest depends less on the headline price than on who you are, where you are and how many pieces you really need.

Who is allowed to buy at wholesale prices?

Wholesalers protect their trade prices with conditions. The typical requirements are:

  • A registered business. textil-grosshandel.eu, one of the largest German blank-apparel wholesalers, states that its offer is aimed exclusively at commercial customers and runs a separate shop for private buyers.
  • A customer account, often with a trade register extract or VAT number, before you can see the real prices.
  • Turnover-based price tiers. textil-grosshandel.eu gives discounts of approx. 7–24 % depending on annual turnover — great for print shops, irrelevant for a one-off order of 60 tees.
  • Prices excluding VAT. The list price is net; VAT is added at checkout or settled through your own VAT return.

Some shops blur the line: textilwaren24.eu sells to both private and business customers with prices including VAT, but delivers only to Germany and Austria. For a club in Ireland, a start-up in Portugal or a school in Finland, the classic wholesale route is often closed or costly once cross-border shipping is added.

No business account, no VAT maths: Marmalade Co. sells the full Fruit of the Loom range from one piece, takes 5 % off from 3 and 10 % off from 5 pieces of the same product, and ships to 11 EU countries for a flat €7 with DHL.

How does VAT work on wholesale orders in the EU?

If you buy as a business from a supplier in another EU country and give a valid EU VAT number, the supplier does not charge VAT; you account for it in your own country under the reverse-charge rules (see the EU's Your Europe portal). That is why wholesale prices are shown net. Two consequences:

  • VAT-registered businesses should compare net prices, because they can reclaim VAT anyway.
  • Clubs, schools, associations and small businesses below the VAT threshold cannot reclaim VAT, so the only fair comparison is the price including VAT. A €2.61 net tee becomes approx. €3.11 in Germany and approx. €3.21 in Ireland before shipping.

Retail prices at Marmalade Co. already include VAT, so the number on the product page is the number you pay, plus €7 shipping per order.

The cheapest route for small businesses, clubs and schools

  1. Under approx. 25 pieces, any buyer: a retailer with quantity discounts. No account, no minimum, and the 10 % discount already applies from 5 pieces.
  2. 25–250 pieces, not VAT-registered (clubs, schools, associations): compare the retailer's VAT-inclusive price against the wholesaler's net price plus VAT and cross-border shipping. Outside Germany and Austria the retailer often wins or comes close.
  3. 250+ pieces, VAT-registered business in the wholesaler's delivery area: open a wholesale account. Ask for the price in your exact colours and sizes, delivery terms to your country and whether reorders are guaranteed in the same shade.
  4. You want it printed: get the printer's price for supplied garments versus garments they source. Many printers accept blanks you buy yourself, which lets you use the cheapest blank route. Our workwear with logo guide covers print versus embroidery costs per employee.

Minimum orders: what to expect

Retailers have no minimum — Marmalade Co. sells from one piece. Wholesalers rarely set a hard minimum quantity but gate prices behind a business account and turnover tiers. Printers are where minimums bite: screen printing usually starts at around 10–25 pieces per design because of set-up costs, while DTG and embroidery shops often print single pieces at a higher price each.

Checklist before you order in volume

  • Fix the model and stick to it for the whole order — Valueweight T (approx. 165 g/m²) for most tees, Classic Hooded Sweat (280 g/m²) for hoodies.
  • Collect sizes by chest measurement and add two or three spares in M and L; our size guide has the full charts.
  • Compare prices on the same basis: same colour, same VAT treatment, shipping to your address.
  • Check delivery time against your deadline: blanks from Marmalade Co. arrive in 2–3 business days; printing adds one to two weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy directly from Fruit of the Loom in Europe?

Not as a consumer or small buyer. Fruit of the Loom International, based in Kaiserslautern, Germany, sells to the trade through distributors and wholesalers, and fruitoftheloom.eu is a catalogue for business customers with an account login rather than an open shop. Individuals, clubs and small businesses buy through wholesalers, print shops or retailers such as Marmalade Co., which sells from one piece with VAT included.

Do I need a VAT number to buy Fruit of the Loom wholesale?

Usually yes, or at least a registered business. Wholesalers such as textil-grosshandel.eu sell exclusively to commercial customers and quote prices excluding VAT. Across EU borders, a valid VAT number lets the supplier invoice without VAT under the reverse-charge rules. Without one, retailers with quantity discounts, where the price already includes VAT, are the practical route.

What is the minimum order for Fruit of the Loom wholesale?

Most wholesalers do not set a fixed minimum quantity but require a business account and give better prices at higher annual turnover. Retailers have no minimum at all: Marmalade Co. sells from one piece and applies 5 % off from 3 and 10 % off from 5 pieces of the same product. Screen printers typically quote from around 10–25 pieces per design.

Is wholesale always cheaper than buying from a retailer?

Not always. Wholesale from-prices of approx. €2.24–2.68 per tee apply to the cheapest colour, often exclude VAT and may only ship within Germany and Austria. Once VAT, colour and cross-border shipping are added, a retailer such as Marmalade Co. at €6.26 per tee from 5 pieces, VAT included with €7 shipping, can be competitive for orders under a few hundred pieces.
