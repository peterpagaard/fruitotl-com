How do Fruit of the Loom kids' sizes work?

Unlike most high-street children's ranges, Fruit of the Loom does not label kids' clothes by age. Each size is a number that corresponds to the child's height in centimetres. That is deliberate: two seven-year-olds can differ by 15 cm, so height is a far more reliable guide than a birthday.

Measure the child standing against a wall without shoes, then pick the size whose height range includes that number. If the child is at the top of a range — or you want the garment to last a second school year — go one size up.

Why is Fruit of the Loom so popular for school and club clothing?

Walk past any European primary-school sports day and you will see the brand. Three things explain it:

  • Price — at €4.95 for a T-shirt, a class of 25 can be kitted out for approx. €111 after the 10 % quantity discount, before printing.

Ordering for a group? Ask for each child's height rather than age, and add two or three extra pieces in the most common sizes (usually 128 and 140) — those run out first. Our guide to class and team hoodies covers timing, print methods and cost per child.

How should you wash Fruit of the Loom kids' clothes?

Children's clothes are washed more than anything else in the house, so care matters more than for adult basics.

  • Wash at 30–40 °C. Cotton tees can shrink a few per cent on a hot first wash; the 80/20 sweats shrink less.
  • Turn printed garments inside out to protect names, logos and class prints.
  • Air dry or tumble on low — a hot tumble cycle is the main cause of a hoodie that suddenly "got small".
  • Buying a size up for growth is fine on hoodies and sweatpants (the cuffs hold the length in); on T-shirts it looks baggy, so stick to the height chart.
