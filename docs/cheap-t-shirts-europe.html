Cheap T-shirts compared — what quality do you get for each euro?

Updated: 2026-09-11

Cheap T-shirts are easy to find; cheap T-shirts that still look right after fifty washes are not. We compare the basics brands you can actually buy across Europe and Ireland — Fruit of the Loom, Gildan and B&C — with fast-fashion tees from H&M and Primark on weight, cotton, price per piece and print suitability, with prices checked in September 2026.

Quick answer

The best value cheap T-shirt in Europe is the Fruit of the Loom Valueweight T (61-036): approx. 165 g/m², 100 % cotton, €6.95 or €6.26 each at 5+ at Marmalade Co., with €7 DHL shipping and 2–3 day delivery. At wholesale list prices (approx., September 2026) Gildan Heavy Cotton 5000 is approx. €7.60 and B&C #E150 approx. €7.60, so Fruit of the Loom is both the cheapest and the easiest to buy at consumer prices. Avoid anything under 140 g/m².

What counts as a cheap T-shirt that is still worth buying?

We use three tests. The T-shirt should be 100 % cotton or close to it (heather colours are the exception), it should weigh at least 145–150 g/m² so it survives regular washing, and it should cost under about €8 for a single piece and under €6.50 when you buy a few. Very few products pass all three, and the ones that do are almost all from the basics brands that supply Europe's print shops: Fruit of the Loom, Gildan and B&C.

Below we compare those three with fast-fashion basics from H&M and Primark on fabric weight, cotton, price per piece and how well they take a print — with prices checked in September 2026.

What quality do you get for each euro?

Under €5: event and one-wash tees

This is the Original T and B&C #E150 territory at wholesale — approx. 145 g/m², fine for a fun run, a festival crew or a promotion that will be worn a handful of times. They are not thin junk, but a lighter fabric shows more through and wears out sooner at the collar.

€5–8: the everyday sweet spot

The Valueweight T (approx. 165 g/m²), Gildan Softstyle and Heavy Cotton and B&C #E190 all live here. This is where price per wash is best: a 165–195 g/m² cotton tee at €6–7 that survives dozens of washes costs less per wear than a €4 tee that is out of shape after ten. The Valueweight uses Belcoro® yarn, which gives a smoother surface for printing and a softer feel than Gildan's open-end cotton on the 5000. For a colour-by-colour look at the range see our Fruit of the Loom T-shirt guide.

€8–15: heavier or branded basics

The Heavy Cotton T at approx. 195 g/m² and premium lines like Stanley/Stella's organic tees. Worth it if you want a thicker, more opaque tee or an organic certificate, not if price is the goal.

Fruit of the Loom vs Gildan: which cheap tee should you pick?

  • Feel: Fruit of the Loom's Belcoro® and ring-spun cotton feels softer than Gildan's open-end Heavy Cotton 5000. Gildan Softstyle closes the gap.
  • Fit: Gildan 5000 is boxy and American-cut; Fruit of the Loom is a slimmer regular European fit. Gildan runs large — size down if you are between sizes.
  • Weight: Gildan 5000 is heavier (approx. 180 g/m²) than the Valueweight (approx. 165), but the Fruit of the Loom Heavy Cotton T (approx. 195) beats both.
  • Price and availability: at wholesale list price the Valueweight is about €1.20 cheaper than Gildan 5000, and at consumer prices Fruit of the Loom is far easier to buy in Europe.

The full comparison, including print results, is in Fruit of the Loom vs Gildan.

Are H&M and Primark T-shirts cheaper?

On the high street, sometimes — Primark in particular sells basic multipacks at very low unit prices, and H&M sells three-packs of regular-fit tees. Neither chain publishes fabric weights, and we could not verify a current EUR price online for either at the time of writing, so we do not list a number. Three practical differences remain: fast-fashion tees cannot be bought blank for printing, colours and cuts change every season so you cannot re-order the same tee next year, and the cheapest multipack styles are usually lighter than 150 g/m². For a plain tee you want to buy again in the same colour, or to print on, the basics brands are the better deal.

How do you get the lowest price on a good T-shirt?

  1. Pick the Valueweight T if price per wash matters most. It is the cheapest model at 165 g/m² and the print-shop standard.
  2. Buy in threes or fives. Marmalade Co. gives 5 % off at 3 pieces and 10 % off at 5, taking the Valueweight to €6.26 each. Buying 10–15 for a team or event drops the price further at wholesalers, but they usually need a business account. Our bulk T-shirt guide covers that route.
  3. Stick to stock colours. White, black, navy and grey are always cheapest; heather and fashion colours cost more.
  4. Count shipping. A flat €7 DHL fee with 2–3 business day delivery to eleven EU countries is easy to compare; marketplace sellers often add more per item.
  5. Use a price guarantee. Marmalade Co. matches the price of an identical product found cheaper elsewhere, so the €6.95 price is effectively a ceiling.

When is a T-shirt too cheap?

Below about €3 per piece at consumer prices you are almost always looking at a no-name tee under 140 g/m², with uneven sizing, a collar that twists after a few washes and noticeable shrinkage. The 145–165 g/m² of the Original T and Valueweight T is the lower limit of "cheap but solid"; 165 g/m² or more gives better print results and longer life. Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest good-quality T-shirt in Europe?

The Fruit of the Loom Valueweight T: approx. 165 g/m², 100 % cotton with Belcoro® yarn, €6.95 at Marmalade Co. and €6.26 each when you buy five or more, with a price guarantee and €7 DHL delivery in 2–3 business days. Wholesalers list it at approx. €6.40 and sell outlet stock lower, but usually only to businesses.

Is Gildan cheaper than Fruit of the Loom for T-shirts?

Not at the Irish wholesaler we checked in September 2026: Gildan Heavy Cotton 5000 listed at approx. €7.60 against approx. €6.40 for the Fruit of the Loom Valueweight T, and Gildan Softstyle matched the Valueweight at approx. €6.40. At consumer prices Fruit of the Loom is far easier to buy in Europe, and it is a slimmer, softer tee.

Are H&M or Primark T-shirts cheaper than Fruit of the Loom?

Their basic multipacks can be cheaper per unit on the high street, but neither chain states fabric weight, we could not verify a current EUR price online, and the cheapest packs are usually lighter than 150 g/m². They also cannot be bought blank for printing or re-ordered in the same colour next year, which is where the basics brands win.

What weight should a cheap T-shirt be?

145–150 g/m² is the lower limit for a tee that holds up to regular washing; 165 g/m² or more gives better opacity, longer life and better print results. The Fruit of the Loom Original T is approx. 145 g/m², the Valueweight approx. 165 and the Heavy Cotton T approx. 195. Avoid anything under 140 g/m².

Do cheap cotton T-shirts shrink?

100 % cotton can shrink a few percent on a hot first wash, whichever brand you buy. Wash at 30–40 °C, avoid hot tumble drying and the shrinkage is minimal. Fruit of the Loom's classic tees are tubular with no side seams, so they do not twist at the sides the way some cheap side-seamed tees do.

Which cheap T-shirt is best for printing?

The Fruit of the Loom Valueweight T is the print-shop standard in Europe thanks to its smooth Belcoro® yarn surface and 16 stock colours. For a heavier, more opaque print base choose the Heavy Cotton T at approx. 195 g/m². Gildan 5000 and B&C #E190 also print well; fast-fashion tees are not sold blank.
