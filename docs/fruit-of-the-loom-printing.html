Fruit of the Loom is the default blank garment in European print shops, and not by accident: tubular tees, ring-spun cotton and stable product codes make it predictable to decorate. Why is Fruit of the Loom the standard blank for printing?

Walk into almost any screen-printing or embroidery workshop in Europe and you will find Fruit of the Loom on the shelf. Printers standardise on it for three practical reasons:

  • A smooth, stable surface. The premium lines use ring-spun / combed cotton, and many sweats use Fruit of the Loom's own Belcoro® yarn, which has fewer loose fibres. Ink and thread sit on a flatter surface, so edges stay sharp.
  • Tubular tees. The classic t-shirts are knitted as a tube with no side seams — an uninterrupted print area on front and back, and no seam to jam under a screen or a heat press.
  • Consistency. The product codes (61-036, 62-208 and so on) have been stable for decades. A reorder six months later matches the first batch in cut, weight and colour.

How to prepare a print-ready order

  1. Pick the model, then the colour. Solid colours print most predictably; check heather colours with your printer.
  2. Collect sizes with the size chart. Adult chest: S 89–94 cm, M 95–100, L 101–106, XL 107–112, 2XL 113–118, 3XL 119–124. Kids by height (104–164). Use our size guide and order one or two spares in M and L.
  3. Supply vector artwork (PDF/SVG/AI) for screen print and embroidery; 300-dpi PNG at final size for DTG.
  4. Decide placement. Standard chest logo approx. 8–10 cm wide; full front approx. 25–30 cm; full back up to 30 cm. Tubular tees have no seam to avoid.
  5. Order blanks early. Marmalade Co. dispatches within 0–1 business day and delivers in 2–3, but the printer's queue is the real bottleneck — allow one to three weeks in autumn.

Caring for printed garments

Print life depends more on the wash than on the ink. Wash printed Fruit of the Loom inside out at 30–40 °C, skip fabric softener on DTG prints, and avoid hot tumble drying — heat is what cracks plastisol and lifts vinyl. 100 % cotton tees can shrink a few percent on a hot first wash, so the 30 °C rule protects both the fit and the design. Treated this way, a screen-printed Valueweight T routinely outlives its design's relevance, and an embroidered polo will survive years of industrial laundering. Frequently asked questions

Which Fruit of the Loom t-shirt is best for screen printing?

The Valueweight T (61-036, approx. 165 g/m²) is the industry standard: 100 % cotton, tubular construction without side seams and a smooth surface that holds plastisol and water-based inks well. The lighter Original T (approx. 145 g/m²) is fine for large event runs, while the Heavy Cotton T (approx. 195 g/m²) suits workwear and long-life merchandise.

Is Fruit of the Loom good for DTG printing?

Yes, as long as you choose a 100 % cotton model and preferably a white or light colour. DTG inks bond to cotton fibres, so the Valueweight T, Original T and Iconic 150 T all work; heather colours contain polyester and can give a duller result. DTG has no set-up cost, which makes it the right choice for one to about 25 pieces or full-colour artwork.

Can Fruit of the Loom hoodies be embroidered?

Very well. The Classic Hooded Sweat (62-208) and Classic Set-In Sweat (62-202) are 280 g/m² fabrics with 80 % cotton / 20 % polyester and Belcoro® yarn, heavy enough to hold a chest logo without puckering. The zip hoodie (62-062) and the piqué polos are also popular for embroidered staff wear. Keep logos simple to limit stitch count and cost.

How much does a printed Fruit of the Loom t-shirt cost?

A one-stop print service such as vistaprint.ie lists a printed Fruit of the Loom Basic Unisex T from about €15 with no minimum. Buying blanks separately — €6.26 per tee from 5 pieces at Marmalade Co., plus €7 shipping — and using a local screen printer is usually cheaper per piece once you pass roughly 25 pieces with a one-colour design.

Screen print or DTG — which lasts longer?

Screen print is the more durable of the two and handles dark garments better thanks to its opaque ink layer. DTG prints last well on light cotton if washed inside out at 30–40 °C without fabric softener or hot tumble drying. Embroidery outlasts both and is the safest choice for garments that go through industrial laundry, such as staff polos.

How many spare blanks should I order for a print run?

Order one or two extra in the most common sizes (M and L) per 25 pieces. Misprints happen, people change size, and reorders of a single tee cost a full shipping fee.
