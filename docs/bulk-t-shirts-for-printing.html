Which t-shirt should you buy in bulk for printing?

For events, merchandise, clubs and campaigns the choice comes down to two Fruit of the Loom models, with a third for special cases:

  • Valueweight T (61-036), approx. 165 g/m² — the default. 100 % cotton, tubular body without side seams, a smooth surface for screen printing and DTG, and the widest colour range. If you are not sure, order this.
  • Original T (61-082), approx. 145 g/m² — lighter and slightly cheaper at wholesale. Fine for one-day events, summer festivals and giveaways where the tee will be worn a handful of times.
  • Heavy Cotton T (61-212), approx. 195 g/m² — thicker, boxier, more durable. Choose it for staff tees or merchandise people will wear for years.

Whatever you choose, keep the whole order on one model: sizing, colour shade and print behaviour then stay identical across the run, and reorders match. Our t-shirt model guide has the full breakdown.

How to split sizes for a group order

The most common bulk mistake is not the model or the shop but the size split. For a mixed adult group with no size list, a typical distribution is roughly 10 % S, 25 % M, 30 % L, 20 % XL, 10 % 2XL and 5 % 3XL — adjust for your audience (student groups skew smaller, trade teams larger). If you can collect sizes, use chest measurements: S 89–94 cm, M 95–100, L 101–106, XL 107–112, 2XL 113–118, 3XL 119–124. Kids' tees run by height: 104, 116, 128, 140, 152, 164.

Step by step: ordering bulk tees for printing

  1. Fix the design and colour count. One or two ink colours keep screen-printing set-up cheap; full-colour artwork points to DTG for runs under about 25 pieces.
  2. Choose the garment colour. Light colours are cheapest to print (no white underbase). Solid colours are more predictable than heathers, which contain polyester.
  3. Get two printer quotes with your quantity and colour count before ordering blanks, so the blank order matches what the printer needs.
  4. Order blanks in one go from the t-shirt collection at Marmalade Co.. Five-plus pieces trigger the 10 % discount; one parcel means one €7 fee. Dispatch is within 0–1 business day and delivery 2–3 business days.
  5. Check the delivery for sizes and colours before handing over to the printer; blanks are easy to exchange, printed tees are not.
  6. Keep a few spares blank for latecomers and misprints.

Which technique fits your run — screen print, DTG, vinyl or embroidery — is covered in our printing guide.

Timing: how long does a bulk order take?

Blanks are the fast part: 2–3 business days from Marmalade Co. Printing is the slow part — expect one to two weeks for a standard screen-printing job and longer in September–November when schools, clubs and companies all order at once. Work backwards from your event date, add a week of buffer, and order blanks as soon as the size list is final.
