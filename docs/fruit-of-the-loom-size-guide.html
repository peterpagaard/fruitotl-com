Fruit of the Loom size guide — charts in cm, how to measure and fit advice

Updated: 2026-09-11

Choosing a size in a Fruit of the Loom T-shirt, sweatshirt or hoodie is easy once you know two things: your chest measurement in cm and how the model is cut. This guide gives you the charts for adults and kids, a three-step way to measure yourself and practical advice on going up or down — whether you are buying one shirt or fifty for a team.

Quick answer

Fruit of the Loom follows a standard European chart. Adult chest in cm (approx.): XS 84–88, S 89–94, M 95–100, L 101–106, XL 107–112, 2XL 113–118, 3XL 119–124, with 4XL 125–130 and 5XL 131–136 on some models. T-shirts have a regular fit; sweatshirts and hoodies are cut slightly roomier for layering. Kids' sizes are by height: 104, 116, 128, 140, 152 and 164 cm. For a close fit go one size down, for layering one size up. Marmalade Co. stocks XS–3XL and kids 104–152.

How do you find your Fruit of the Loom size in 3 steps?

  1. Measure your chest. Wrap a tape measure horizontally around the widest part of your chest, under the armpits, over a thin T-shirt or bare skin. Keep it snug but not tight, and breathe normally.
  2. Find the row. Compare the number in cm with the "Chest" column in the chart for the product type you are buying — T-shirts and sweats differ slightly.
  3. Adjust for the fit you want. A close fit: one size down. A relaxed fit or a layer underneath: one size up. Between two sizes: go up, because cotton can shrink a little.

If you already own a Fruit of the Loom garment that fits well, the fastest method is to lay it flat and compare its width and length with the tables below.

Does Fruit of the Loom run small or large?

Neither, by European standards. The brand follows a conventional chart: T-shirts have a regular fit that is neither slim nor oversized, and sweats are cut a touch roomier. Compared with fashion brands that cut slim (many Iconic-style or retail tees), Fruit of the Loom classic tees feel slightly more generous; compared with Gildan's boxier US-style cut, they feel slightly trimmer. Our Fruit of the Loom vs Gildan comparison goes into that difference.

  • Want it fitted? One size down — or choose the Iconic 150 T, which is cut slimmer by design.
  • Want it loose or layered? One size up.
  • Between two sizes? Go up. A 100 % cotton tee can shrink a few per cent on a hot first wash, and a slightly loose shirt looks better than a tight one.
  • Tall with a normal chest? Stay with your chest size; length grows by about 2 cm per size, so going up two sizes for length makes the body too wide.

How do you pick sizes for a whole group?

Ordering for a team, a class or a company is where guessing goes wrong. Instead of asking everyone for "S, M or L", collect chest measurements in cm and assign sizes from the chart — people are surprisingly bad at knowing their own size, and men in particular tend to under-report. Then:

  • Add 5–10 % extra in M and L — they run out first.
  • Order one size up for anyone who will wear the garment over a uniform or workwear layer.
  • For mixed adult and kids' groups, note that kids' 164 and adult XS overlap; use height for the child and chest for the adult.
  • Buy in one batch: quantity discounts at Marmalade Co. are 5 % from 3 pieces and 10 % from 5 pieces, and a single delivery means one €7 shipping fee.

Washing, shrinkage and keeping the size you bought

Most Fruit of the Loom tees are 100 % cotton and can lose a few per cent in length on a hot first wash; sweats in 80 % cotton / 20 % polyester shrink less. To keep the fit:

  • Wash at 30–40 °C, inside out if printed.
  • Air dry or tumble on low — a hot tumble cycle is the usual cause of shrinkage.
  • Reshape while damp and hang by the hem rather than the shoulders.

Order the right size, treat the garment gently, and a Fruit of the Loom basic will fit the same way after fifty washes as it did on day one.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Fruit of the Loom sizes in cm?

Adult chest measurements (approx.): XS 84–88 cm, S 89–94, M 95–100, L 101–106, XL 107–112, 2XL 113–118 and 3XL 119–124, with 4XL 125–130 and 5XL 131–136 on selected models. Sweatshirts and hoodies use slightly wider ranges (S 91–97, M 98–104, L 105–111). Kids are sized by height from 104 to 164 cm.

Does Fruit of the Loom run small?

No. Fruit of the Loom follows a standard European chart with a regular fit on T-shirts and a slightly roomier cut on sweatshirts and hoodies. Only the Iconic 150 T is cut slimmer. If you want a close fit, take one size down; for layering or a relaxed look, take one size up; if between sizes, go up.

How do I measure myself for a Fruit of the Loom T-shirt?

Wrap a tape measure horizontally around the widest part of your chest, under the armpits, snug but not tight. Compare the result in cm with the chart: for example 95–100 cm is a size M, 101–106 cm a size L. Alternatively lay a well-fitting shirt flat and measure its width armpit to armpit — an M is approx. 52 cm wide.

Should I size up for a Fruit of the Loom hoodie?

Usually not. The Classic Hooded Sweat and other sweats are already cut roomier than the T-shirts so that they fit over another layer, so your normal letter size works for a standard layered fit. Size up only if you want a deliberately oversized look or plan to wear it over thick workwear or a fleece.

How do Fruit of the Loom kids' sizes work?

Kids' garments are labelled by height in cm: 104 (approx. 3–4 years), 116 (5–6), 128 (7–8), 140 (9–11), 152 (12–13) and 164 (14–15). Measure the child without shoes and pick the size whose range covers that height; if they are near the top of a range, choose the next size to allow for growth.

Will a Fruit of the Loom T-shirt shrink after washing?

A 100 % cotton tee can shrink a few per cent — mostly in length — if the first wash is hot or it is tumble dried on high. Washed at 30–40 °C and air dried, shrinkage is minimal. The 80/20 cotton-polyester sweats are more stable. If you are between sizes, choosing the larger one covers any shrinkage.
